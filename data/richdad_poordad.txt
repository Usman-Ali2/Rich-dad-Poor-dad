RICH DAD POOR DAD
By Robert T. Kiyosaki
A Summary of Key Lessons and Concepts

INTRODUCTION

Rich Dad Poor Dad is Robert Kiyosaki's story of growing up with two dads — his real father (poor dad) and the father of his best friend (rich dad) — and the ways in which both men shaped his thoughts about money and investing. The book explodes the myth that you need to earn a high income to be rich and challenges the belief that your house is an asset. The book teaches you what the rich teach their kids about money that the middle class and poor do not.

LESSON 1: THE RICH DON'T WORK FOR MONEY

Most people have a price. They have a price because of human emotions named fear and greed. Fear of being without money motivates them to work hard, and then once they get that paycheck, greed or desire starts them thinking about all the wonderful things money can buy. The pattern is then set. The pattern of get up, go to work, pay bills, get up, go to work, pay bills.

People's lives are forever controlled by two emotions: fear and greed. Offer them more money, and they continue the cycle by also increasing their spending. This is what Robert calls the Rat Race.

The poor and the middle class work for money. The rich have money work for them. Once a person stops searching for information and self-knowledge, ignorance sets in. This temporary comfort of not questioning leads to suffering.

It is fear that keeps most people working at a job: the fear of not paying their bills, the fear of being fired, the fear of not having enough money, and the fear of starting over. That is the price of studying only to learn a profession and never learning how to have money work for you.

Money is not taught in schools. Schools focus on scholastic and professional skills, but not on financial skills. This explains how smart bankers, doctors, and accountants who earned excellent grades in school may still struggle financially all their lives.

LESSON 2: WHY TEACH FINANCIAL LITERACY

Rich people acquire assets. The poor and middle class acquire liabilities, but they think they are assets. The rich focus on their asset column while everyone else focuses on their income statement.

An asset is something that puts money in your pocket. A liability is something that takes money out of your pocket. Rich dad kept it simple — the difference between assets and liabilities is as simple as that.

If you want to be rich, simply spend your life buying assets. If you want to be poor or middle class, spend your life buying liabilities. It is not knowing the difference that causes most of the financial struggle in the real world.

Most people work for everyone else but themselves. They work first for the owners of the company, then for the government through taxes, and then for the bank through mortgage and credit card debt.

Financial intelligence is made up of knowledge from four broad areas: accounting, investing, understanding markets, and the law. Accounting is financial literacy or the ability to read numbers. Investing is the science of money making money. Understanding markets is the science of supply and demand. The law is awareness of accounting corporate rules, state and national regulations.

The single most powerful asset we all have is our mind. If it is trained well, it can create enormous wealth. An untrained mind can also create extreme poverty that can crush a family for generations.

LESSON 3: MIND YOUR OWN BUSINESS

Financial struggle is often directly the result of people working all their life for someone else. Many people will have nothing at the end of their working days. Keep your daytime job, but start buying real assets, not liabilities or personal effects that have no real value once you get them home.

Real assets include businesses that do not require your presence, stocks, bonds, income-generating real estate, notes, royalties from intellectual property, and anything else that has value, produces income, or appreciates, and has a ready market.

What the lower income parents taught their children: go to school, get good grades, get a good job. What the rich teach their kids: mind your own business, build assets, make money work for you.

McDonald's is a perfect example of a business that made Ray Kroc rich. It is not the best hamburger, but the system is. The system is what made Ray Kroc rich, not the hamburger. What is the system? The system runs the business, not the people.

LESSON 4: THE HISTORY OF TAXES AND THE POWER OF CORPORATIONS

Corporations were originally created as a way to help build empires. In the early days, kings and queens owned corporations and used them to explore and acquire wealth. Today, corporations are used by wealthy individuals to protect their assets and avoid paying taxes.

The rich use corporations to protect their assets. Before paying taxes, the rich can legally pay for their expenses, protect their assets from lawsuits, and pass on wealth to their heirs. The poor and middle class earn money, pay taxes on it, and then spend what is left over. The rich earn money, spend it, and pay taxes on what is left. This is the corporate tax advantage.

Financial IQ is made up of: accounting, investing, understanding markets, and the law. Financial IQ helps you solve financial problems. The main management skills needed for success include management of cash flow, management of systems, and management of people.

LESSON 5: THE RICH INVENT MONEY

Often in the real world, it's not the smart who get ahead, but the bold. Real financial intelligence is creating more money with less money. This requires creativity. You must be able to recognize opportunities others miss, raise money for investment, organize smart people, and find deals that profit everyone.

Financial intelligence is the synergy of accounting, investing, market knowledge, and law. Combine those four technical skills and making money with money is easier than most people believe.

The most important specialized skills are sales and marketing. Communication skills are essential. Writing, speaking, and negotiating are all key to success. The most important specialized skill is the ability to sell — to communicate to another human being.

Most people, given more money, simply get into more debt. This is why more money rarely solves someone's money problems. Financial intelligence solves money problems. It is what you know that is your greatest wealth. It is what you don't know that is your greatest risk.

LESSON 6: WORK TO LEARN, DON'T WORK FOR MONEY

Most people need only to learn and master one more skill and their income would jump exponentially. The world is filled with talented poor people. They are poor or struggle financially or earn less than they are capable of, not because of what they know but because of what they do not know.

Job security meant everything to his poor dad. Learning meant everything to his rich dad. Poor dad believed in working for security. Rich dad believed in learning to create your own security.

Life is the best teacher of all. Most of the time, life does not talk to you. It just shoves you around. Each shove is life saying 'Wake up. There's something I want you to learn.' Most people are overspecialized. They stay in one industry their whole lives and become excellent at one thing. But they miss the broader financial education.

The main reason people struggle financially is because they have spent years in school but learned nothing about money. They know how to work hard, but they don't know how to have money work hard for them.

LESSON 7: OVERCOMING OBSTACLES

The main reason people fail to achieve financial freedom is fear. The five main reasons financially literate people are not rich are: fear, cynicism, laziness, bad habits, and arrogance.

Fear of losing money is real, and everyone has it. Even the rich have fear. The difference is how they handle fear. The fear of losing money is real. Robert's rich dad's way of handling fear was, "I have never met a rich person who has never lost money, but I have met many poor people who have never lost a dime."

Cynicism and doubt keep most people poor and playing it safe. The most common form of laziness is staying busy. Being busy can be a form of laziness — lazy thinking and indiscriminate action. People who stay busy often avoid the important issues of their financial lives.

Bad habits control our behavior more than we know. Most of our habits were developed as young children. Bad financial habits lead to financial failure. Good financial habits lead to financial success and freedom.

Arrogance is ego plus ignorance. Many people use arrogance to hide their ignorance. When you know you are ignorant in a subject, start educating yourself by finding an expert in the field or a book on the subject.

LESSON 8: GETTING STARTED

There are ten steps to awakening your financial genius: find a reason greater than reality (the power of spirit), make daily choices (the power of choice), choose friends carefully (the power of association), master a formula and then learn a new one (the power of learning quickly), pay yourself first (the power of self-discipline), pay your brokers well (the power of good advice), be an Indian giver (the power of getting something for nothing), use assets to buy luxuries (the power of focus), choose heroes (the power of myth), and teach and you shall receive (the power of giving).

To find great deals, you need to be in the market. You need to be looking actively. You need to be practicing the patterns and systems you are learning. Most great deals are not advertised. You have to know where to look.

Every day I have a choice to be financially free. I can choose to be guided by fear and the opinions of others or I can choose to have financial literacy guide me. It is never too late to start learning. It is never too late to start doing things right.

ASSETS AND LIABILITIES

Assets include: stocks, bonds, mutual funds, income-producing real estate, notes, royalties from intellectual property, and anything else that has value, produces income or appreciates, and has a ready market.

Liabilities include: mortgages on personal homes (if the house doesn't generate income), car loans, credit card debt, student loans for non-business education, and consumer goods bought on credit.

Rich dad said that a house is not an asset. Most people believe their home is their biggest asset, but rich dad said a house is a liability because it takes money out of your pocket every month. For a house to be an asset, it must generate income (like a rental property).

The middle class buys liabilities they think are assets. They buy a new house, then the mortgage means more to pay. Then they fill the house with new furniture bought on credit. Then the kids are born, and the kids need a college fund. The income goes up but so does the debt.

Cash flow is the most important concept in financial literacy. Assets produce positive cash flow (money coming in). Liabilities create negative cash flow (money going out). The rich focus on building their asset column to create cash flow. The poor and middle class focus on their income column.

INVESTING

Investing is not risky. Not knowing what you are doing is risky. Investing requires financial education. Most people know the prices of things but not the value. They know what things cost but don't know their worth.

The key principle of investing is to find an asset that creates cash flow and gives you a return on your investment. Real estate can be a good investment if bought correctly — when the numbers make sense. Never buy a property if you cannot generate positive cash flow from it.

Stock market investing requires learning to analyze companies, understanding financial statements, knowing when to buy and when to sell. Dollar-cost averaging is a technique where you invest a fixed amount regularly regardless of price, buying more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high.

Diversification means spreading investments across different asset classes (real estate, stocks, bonds, businesses) to reduce risk. Don't put all your eggs in one basket. But also don't diversify for the sake of diversifying.

FINANCIAL FREEDOM

Financial freedom means having enough income from your assets to cover your expenses without needing to work. When your passive income exceeds your expenses, you are financially free.

Passive income comes from: rental properties, dividend stocks, interest from bonds and savings, royalties, business income where you don't need to be present. Building passive income takes time and financial education.

The path to financial freedom: first, learn financial literacy. Second, begin acquiring assets. Third, reinvest income from assets to buy more assets. Fourth, keep your expenses low until your asset income exceeds your expenses. Fifth, you are financially free.

Rich dad's three-part formula for financial freedom: learn accounting, learn investing, and take action. Most people know what to do but fail to take action due to fear and doubt.

MONEY MANAGEMENT

Pay yourself first is a powerful concept. When you receive income, set aside a percentage for investing before paying bills. This forces you to find ways to pay your bills even when money is tight, which develops financial creativity and discipline.

The 10/10/10 rule: save 10% of your income, invest 10% of your income in assets, give 10% to charity. This simple rule, practiced consistently, leads to financial security over time.

Budgeting is important. Know where your money goes. Track your spending. Most people are shocked when they see how much they spend on things that take money out of their pocket without putting any back in.

Credit card debt is one of the biggest obstacles to wealth. High interest rates on credit cards make it very difficult to build wealth. Pay off credit card debt as soon as possible. If you must use credit cards, pay them off in full each month.

ENTREPRENEURSHIP

Being an employee gives you the least tax advantages. Being self-employed gives you more. Owning a business gives you even more. Owning a business and investing gives you the most tax advantages.

An entrepreneur is someone who creates value for others and gets paid for that value. Entrepreneurship requires courage, financial education, and the ability to handle failure and learn from it.

The most successful businesses solve problems. If you want to be a successful entrepreneur, find a real problem that many people have and create a solution that people will pay for. The bigger the problem you solve, the bigger the potential business.

A business owner must understand basic financial concepts: revenue, expenses, profit, cash flow, assets, liabilities, balance sheets, and income statements. Without this knowledge, it is difficult to run a successful business.

FINANCIAL EDUCATION

Financial education is not taught in schools. Most people graduate without knowing how to balance a checkbook, understand a tax return, read a financial statement, or make sound investment decisions.

The four pillars of financial education are: accounting (knowing how to read financial statements), investing (knowing how money works), understanding markets (understanding supply and demand), and legal knowledge (understanding tax advantages and corporate structures).

Reading financial books, attending seminars, finding mentors, and practice are all ways to increase financial education. The best investment you can make is in your own education.
